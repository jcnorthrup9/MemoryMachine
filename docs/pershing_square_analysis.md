# Pershing Square (1992 Legorreta) — Spatial Analysis

## Design Logic
The site is dominated by a postmodern language of isolated, monumental objects (attractors) placed within a largely undifferentiated and hostile hardscape. Circulation is heavily constrained by infrastructural barriers (garage ramps) and low walls, creating a fortress-like condition that disconnects the park from its urban context.

## Erasure Targets
*   Garage Ramp (Olive St)
*   Garage Ramp (Hill St)
*   Hardscape Plaza

## Zones
*   **A: Campanile Tower** (Main Attractor)
*   **B: Hardscape Plaza** (Hostile Ground Plane)
*   **C: Aqueduct Fountain** (Water Feature)
*   **D: Raised Planter** (Green Space (Isolated))

## Paths
*   **P1: Garage Ramp (Olive St)** (Barrier / Vehicular)
*   **P2: Garage Ramp (Hill St)** (Barrier / Vehicular)
*   **P3: Diagonal 'Earthquake' Path** (Primary Pedestrian)

## Key Spatial Relationships
*   Garage ramps (P1, P2) create hard, hostile edges that sever the park from street-level pedestrian flow.
*   The Campanile Tower (A) acts as a singular, isolated vertical monument within a vast, empty hardscape (B).
*   Circulation is fragmented by low walls and elevation changes, forcing movement along the prescribed diagonal path (P3).
