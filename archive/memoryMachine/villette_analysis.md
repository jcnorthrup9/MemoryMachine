# Parc de la Villette — Spatial Analysis

**Design Logic (overall)**
The agent correctly identifies Tschumi's core strategy: a "park of fragments" where a dot-matrix of attractors (the 26 red follies) is overlaid onto an organic landscape rather than integrated into it. The three independent layers — points (follies), lines (promenades/canals), and surfaces (themed gardens) — operate simultaneously without a single organizing hierarchy.

**Zones identified (12)**

*   Two major green surface areas forming the naturalistic matrix
*   Two water bodies: the Canal de l'Ourcq (linear edge / B1) and the Canal Saint-Denis tributary (curvilinear / B2)
*   Five main attractors (the larger follies + the Grande Halle de la Villette / Cité de la Musique)
*   Two minor attractors (secondary follies at pathway intersections)
*   One rectangular structure (E) — the Grande Halle, acting as a built anchor

**Key Spatial Relationships**

*   Follies as distributed focal points — no single center; visitors are pulled in multiple directions simultaneously, creating an exploratory rather than processional experience
*   The linear promenade (P1) as a spine — connects the rectangular anchor (Grande Halle) to the dispersed attractor network across both green fields
*   Curvilinear paths vs. rectilinear elements — the organic circulation of the thematic gardens deliberately resists the rigidity of the folly grid
*   Water as edge-definer — the canal (B1) creates a hard linear boundary that separates zones; the organic water body (B2) dissolves into the landscape
*   Streets as park boundary — the vehicular edge (S1/S2 = Av. Jean Jaurès / Av. Corentin Cariou) creates a clean severance, making the interior a pedestrian-only world

**Relevance to Pershing Square**
Tschumi's logic is directly relevant here: the folly grid answers the same problem you have at Pershing — how do you distribute program and wayfinding across a large flat hardscape without forcing a single axis or monument? The follies don't contain program; they mark it. Each one is a 10m³ red steel frame that can become a bar, a workshop, a lookout.
