## Design Logic

The dominant spatial logic is a **"park of fragments"** — an event-driven landscape where a grid-like dot-matrix of *attractors* (follies, buildings) is overlaid onto a fluid, organic landscape of green spaces and water features. Pedestrian pathways, varying from rectilinear to curvilinear, weave through this matrix, connecting diverse programmatic nodes.

This creates a **non-hierarchical, exploratory experience** — encouraging discovery and offering a variety of distinct spatial experiences rather than a singular monumental focus. The interplay between formal geometric elements and informal natural forms generates dynamic tension and visual interest.

Tschumi's strategy operates through **three independent layers** that do not subordinate to one another:
- **Points** — the 26 red steel follies on a 120m grid
- **Lines** — the Promenade Cinématique and canals
- **Surfaces** — the themed gardens and open lawns

## Spatial Relationships

1. The **linear central pathway (P1) serves as a primary spine**, connecting the Grande Halle (E) and the distributed attractor network across both green fields
2. **Main attractors are distributed across the site** rather than massed at a single centre — establishing a network of destinations that encourages exploration
3. **Curvilinear paths (P2, P3) gently traverse the naturalistic green spaces**, contrasting with the rectilinear folly grid
4. The **Grande Halle (E) acts as a built anchor** — housing a main attractor and serving as intersection point for primary pedestrian routes
5. **Water features are integrated as distinct spatial devices** — B1 creates a hard linear edge; B2 introduces organic form within the green matrix
6. **Streets (S1, S2) define clean vehicular boundaries**, creating a largely pedestrian-focused interior realm
7. **Minor attractors (D1, D2) are placed at green space edges**, offering localised points of interest along secondary pathways
8. **Green spaces (A1, A2) form the dominant natural matrix** — into which built elements, water features, and circulation paths are embedded

## Program Notes

- **Passive recreation** within the extensive green surfaces
- **Active circulation and exploration** facilitated by the pathway network
- **Cultural, educational, entertainment** programs at the Main Attractors — follies as flexible containers (bar, workshop, lookout, info point)
- **Contemplation and ecological study** at the water features
- **Social gathering** at both main and minor attractor nodes

---

## Scale Notes

The park operates at multiple simultaneous scales: individual follies are human-scaled 10m³ frames, the 120m folly grid is territorial, and the overall 55-hectare park is monumental. This multi-scalar operation means the space never fully belongs to one register — it is always both intimate and vast depending on where you stand.

## Relevance to Pershing Square

> Tschumi's logic directly addresses the same problem at Pershing: **how do you distribute program and wayfinding across a large flat hardscape without forcing a single axis or monument?**

The follies don't *contain* program — they *mark* it. Each 10m³ red steel frame can become a bar, a workshop, a lookout, or remain empty as pure spatial signal. This is the **kit-of-parts logic** already captured in `logic/rhino_precedent_pass.md` under `FOLLY_GRID`.

Applied to Pershing:
- A grid of lightweight steel frames at ~30m intervals could re-establish legible zones without committing to fixed program
- The "three layers" logic (points / lines / surfaces) maps onto Pershing's existing conditions: the campanile and sphere (points), the colonnade and aqueduct wall (lines), the open plaza slab (surface)
- Unlike the 55ha Villette, Pershing's 0.5ha demands compression — fewer follies, tighter grid, but the same distributed-destination logic could break the deadzone quality of the current hardscape
